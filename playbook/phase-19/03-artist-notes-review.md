Ryuichi Sakamoto kept composing until just before his death. In the last pieces he wrote you can really hear the sighs of a whole life, one after another. A sigh can't be simply defined by any word; a sigh is eternal, and that feeling of life being lived is moving. Connected to that source, day by day bringing out that unsteady feeling at the bottom, perhaps someone will really hear such vastness.  
At his last concert, what he played were not perfect notes… they were trembling notes, one by one, yet unerringly accurate to his present moment.

I suddenly realized that if I can say these words from the bottom of my heart, perhaps I'll have a chance to invite more people to join on that website.

This period stopped because of my cat's tumor and my own surgery; life is always in danger.

When I sing and hear the ensemble in the space, my breath becomes very, very long. If I focus only on my own breathing, it becomes short and shallow; even if I wish it were longer, the only thing we can do is to give more space and listen to the ensemble.

66 asked me what I would do if I couldn't communicate in a group. I said it's my intuition: art gives us space, and whenever I have words I can't put into language, I can turn them into a ritual or an action. But it's the same; it still needs that moment of opening one's mouth. I said that moment is an unstructured moment, a subtle sense of danger and a space of experienced failure, and the space of failure is very important. Right now I'm made almost entirely of all kinds of continuous failure. I'm willing to fail completely in every moment, which I was not willing to do before.
